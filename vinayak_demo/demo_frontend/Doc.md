Flight simulation is the process of recreating the behavior and experience of flying an aircraft within a virtual environment. It uses mathematical models to simulate how an aircraft responds to aerodynamic forces such as lift, drag, thrust, and gravity while taking into account environmental conditions like wind, air pressure, temperature, turbulence, and weather. Depending on its purpose, a flight simulator may use simplified physics for entertainment or highly accurate aerodynamic models for professional pilot training and aerospace research.

At the heart of a flight simulator is the flight dynamics model, which calculates the aircraft's motion in six degrees of freedom: forward and backward, left and right, up and down, along with roll, pitch, and yaw. Pilot inputs through the throttle, elevator, ailerons, rudder, flaps, and other controls continuously alter the forces acting on the aircraft. These calculations are performed many times every second, allowing the simulator to realistically update the aircraft's position, orientation, speed, and altitude while maintaining smooth and stable flight.

Modern flight simulators extend beyond aircraft physics by modelling realistic environments and onboard systems. They simulate terrain, airports, navigation aids, air traffic, cockpit instruments, weather conditions, and even system failures such as engine malfunctions or hydraulic problems. Many advanced simulators also include autopilot systems, flight management computers, and real-world navigation procedures, providing an experience that closely resembles operating an actual aircraft.

Flight simulation has become an essential tool in both education and industry. Commercial airlines and military organizations use high-fidelity simulators to train pilots and practice emergency procedures in a safe and cost-effective environment. Aerospace engineers use simulations to evaluate aircraft designs, test control systems, and study flight performance before physical prototypes are built. Researchers also rely on flight simulation for developing autonomous aircraft, drones, and advanced guidance algorithms. As computing technology continues to improve, flight simulators are becoming increasingly realistic, making them invaluable for training, research, engineering, and entertainment alike.
